# Beautiful Names: review of all 99 practical responses

16 October 2026 · edition 1.3

## Scope

Reviewed every “A way to respond” passage alongside its Name’s divine meaning, personal-response section, everyday observation, and reflection question. Revised 93 practices and retained six whose direction was already clear and appropriate. Each practice is at most 39 words; all readings remain within the existing four-minute estimate limit.

The previous edition often repeated “ask Allah, then do one good thing” or a return to an unspecified neglected responsibility. The revised practices give the reader a recognisable occasion, a manageable response, and a connection to Allah through the particular Name. Practical action includes worship, attentive praise, and learning as well as conduct toward other people.

## Editorial decisions

- **Knowledge and attention:** As-Sami‘ invites a private, plain prayer; Al-Basir careful work beyond public notice; Ar-Raqib a return to remembrance between tasks; Ash-Shahid an account limited to what was witnessed; Al-Muhsi follow-through on an overlooked commitment; Al-Khabir honest intention without endless self-examination.
- **Forgiveness and return:** Al-Ghaffar addresses recurrence and planning a different response to a trigger; Al-Ghafur complete honesty in private prayer; Al-Halim an overdue apology while time remains; At-Tawwab acting on an awakening; Al-‘Afuw seeking pardon while willingly releasing a minor personal grievance.
- **Care:** Ar-Rahim asks what would help; Al-Latif considers timing, privacy, and consent; Ar-Ra’uf leaves room for questions without embarrassment; An-Nafi‘ checks whether help actually benefited its recipient. These remain responses to Allah’s mercy and care, not claims to possess His perfection.
- **Strength and reliance:** Al-Qawiyy directs received ability toward useful service; Al-Matin invites a sustainable commitment; Al-Hasib appropriate help; Al-Qayyum entrusting care while resting; Al-Wakil responsible action without claiming the outcome.
- **Giving:** Al-Wahhab gives without reminders of debt; Al-Wasi‘ welcomes another person’s blessing; Al-Karim asks without needing an introduction; Al-Mughni supports another person’s ability rather than cultivating their dependence.
- **Rank and nearness:** Al-Muqaddim gives prayer a place before optional activity. Al-Mu’akhkhir asks for acceptance and nearness as favour after worship, without treating the deed as an entitlement to rank. Al-Khafid, Ar-Rafi‘, Al-Mu‘izz, and Al-Mudhill retain their distinct concern with truth, nearness, honour, and freedom from craving.
- **Transcendence:** Created examples remain occasions for recognising received gifts and limits. Practices involving imagination do not ask the reader to picture Allah. The responses do not turn learning an attribute into a claim to divine status.
- **Tone and agency:** Responses offer limited acts rather than large projects. Prayer need not be polished, a particular feeling is not required, and finite strength is acknowledged. The pardon response is limited to a minor grievance the reader is ready to release; it does not demand surrendering another person’s rights.

These are optional original companion suggestions. They introduce no recitation counts, promised outcomes, completion requirements, or transmitted devotional formulas. Shared meanings remain connected rather than being given invented doctrinal differences for the sake of novelty.
