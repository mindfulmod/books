# Beautiful Names: editorial review of all 99 readings

16 October 2026 · edition 1.2

## Scope and result

Reviewed the meaning, everyday observation, reflection question, and practical response for every Name. Revised 55 observations and 93 questions. Kept the observations that already offered a useful, particular point of attention. The first two theological explanations and Arabic source excerpts remain the basis of each reading.

The main repetition was conceptual: many distinct sentences ended at the same gratitude, helping, responsibility, or relinquishing-control prompt. The revised observations give more precise attention to Allah’s attributes. The questions invite recognition, praise, prayer, trust, or honest examination in response to the particular Name; they need not prescribe another activity because the optional practical response already follows.

The edition still contains related meanings. Shared attributes have not been split into invented doctrines merely to make each reading sound different. Al-Majid and Al-Maajid explicitly return to a shared meaning. Paired Names continue to illuminate each other.

## Main editorial decisions

- **Forgiveness:** Al-Ghaffar attends to repeated return, Al-Ghafur to the fullness of forgiveness with complete knowledge of the wrong, Al-‘Afuw to pardon beyond concealment, and At-Tawwab to Allah’s opening the way of repentance before the servant returns. Al-Halim concerns knowledgeable, powerful forbearance; Al-Muntaqim restores attention to accountability.
- **Knowledge:** Al-‘Alim concerns knowledge never acquired from news; Al-Khabir attends to inward reality; As-Sami‘ and Al-Basir avoid bodily limits; Ar-Raqib concerns uninterrupted watchfulness; Ash-Shahid complete witness; Al-Muhsi exact knowledge of each detail and the whole.
- **Power:** Al-Qawiyy and Al-Matin offer different points of attention within complete strength: no task exposing weakness, and sustaining without exhaustion. Al-Qadir and Al-Muqtadir retain their related meaning while considering will and independence from assistance.
- **Giving and dependence:** The bread’s provision, nourishment actually reaching its bearer, help sufficient through several means, generosity approached without an introduction, and benefit actually received now have different observations. Gratitude leads beyond the gift to its Giver.
- **Rank and nearness:** Al-Mu’akhkhir’s former delayed-plans example did not adequately serve the reading’s central explanation. Its new observation concerns rank determined by Allah, nearness as favour, and the impossibility of judging another soul from circumstances. Al-Muqaddim likewise distinguishes advancement toward Allah from worldly precedence.
- **Transcendence:** Distinctions among holiness, soundness, greatness, exaltation, and hiddenness remain grounded in the existing explanations. Created examples indicate gifts or limits; they do not picture Allah or make His perfection a scaled-up human quality.
- **Justice and difficulty:** The questions do not assign a secret purpose to a particular suffering, identify another person’s rank, equate prosperity with approval, or excuse human injustice.
- **Tone:** Reduced repeated two-part assignments and accusatory either/or questions. Questions remain brief and personal, with Allah named or unambiguously referred to throughout.

## Verification and limits

All 99 proposed observations and questions were reviewed as prose, including the retained observations and the connections among related Names. Automated checks verify coverage, unique strings, length, reading duration, search destinations, and saved-passage compatibility; string uniqueness is not evidence of distinct meaning. The observations contain at most 52 words and the questions at most 25. The changes preserve the existing section and paragraph anchors, and do not rewrite personal notes or previously saved passage snapshots.

Validation: the production build, all 52 Beautiful Names tests, and the shared library checks passed. The local production preview was reviewed at 390 × 844 in daylight (Al-Ghaffar and Al-Ghafur) and 320 × 740 at night (Al-Mu’akhkhir). The revised questions rendered without horizontal overflow, the reflection jump left a 44-pixel offset, and following the connected reading opened at the top.

This is an original editorial companion, not a new translation or independent scholarly certification. The existing source notes and pending scholarly-review disclosure continue to apply.
