## Files

Alt text is already written into `src/book08.ts` and is what each plate has to depict. It is reproduced here as the brief. This book has three journeys.

**Set note — important.** No Arabic text and no legible script anywhere in this set. Books in these plates must be shown closed, covered, or from an angle where the page cannot be read. The subject is what a reader brings to a text and what he may take from it, and rendering the text itself would put an interpretation on the plate.

### 1. `book08-clothed-letters.jpg` + `book08-clothed-letters-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 01 — What am I actually holding?
- **Accent colour** `#278d91`
- **Must depict** A closed book on a plain lectern with a folded cloth laid over it, the cloth thin enough to show the shape beneath.
- **Argument it carries** The speech is a pre-eternal attribute conveyed within the folds of letters — and the clothing is a mercy, not a reduction, since without it no throne or earth would have withstood the hearing. The cloth must read as *protective and deliberate*, finely woven and carefully placed, never as concealment or neglect. The shape beneath must be plainly discernible.

### 2. `book08-addressed.jpg` + `book08-addressed-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 02 — What does reading actually require?
- **Accent colour** `#bf7a35`
- **Must depict** An opened letter lying on a table with its envelope beside it, the name on the envelope turned away from view.
- **Argument it carries** Specification: taking every address in the text to be addressed to you. The letter must be *open and evidently read* — this is a stance already taken, not a decision pending. The envelope's name turned away is what lets the viewer supply himself as the recipient. No legible writing on either.

### 3. `book08-two-lamps.jpg` + `book08-two-lamps-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 03 — How much am I allowed to find in it?
- **Accent colour** `#c25f50`
- **Must depict** A single lamp on a desk lighting an open book, and a second lamp behind the reader's chair casting a shadow across the page.
- **Argument it carries** The two aspects of the prohibition: a reading produced by what the reader already held, versus one the text led him to. The desk lamp lights the page; the lamp behind throws the reader's own shadow onto it. The chair must be empty — the shadow is cast by something outside the frame, which is the whole point. The page must be angled so nothing on it can be read.

### `assets/system/book08-ten-manners.jpg` (+ `-thumb.jpg`)

**Journey 04 — "The ten physical manners"**
An open book resting on a low wooden reading stand, a folded cloth beside it,
in flat even daylight. Nothing else in frame — no hands, no figure, no room
detail competing for attention. The pages should read as pages without any
text being legible.

Carries: the ten outward manners of recitation — posture, pace, division,
voice. The plate has to feel *set up and waiting*, because every one of the ten
is about how you approach the book before and while you read it.

Colour anchor: `#a97837`.
