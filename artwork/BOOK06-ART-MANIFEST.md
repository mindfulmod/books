## Files

Alt text is already written into `src/book06.ts` and is what each plate has to depict. It is reproduced here as the brief. This is the shortest book in the quarter and has three journeys rather than five.

**Set note.** Nothing here should look like deprivation or endurance. Ghazali's argument is that the fast aims at a quality — self-sufficiency, in imitation of beings who have no appetites — so emptiness in these plates is a capacity, not a hardship.

### 1. `book06-three-degrees.jpg` + `book06-three-degrees-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 01 — Which fast am I actually keeping?
- **Accent colour** `#278d91`
- **Must depict** Three plain earthenware bowls of the same size set in a row, the first empty, the second empty and covered, the third empty and turned face down.
- **Argument it carries** Three degrees graded by which organ is restrained, not by how strictly. All three bowls are equally empty — that is the point, since the degrees are not intensities of one fast. What differs is only how far the closing goes: open, covered, inverted. Identical bowls, identical light.

### 2. `book06-the-feedbag.jpg` + `book06-the-feedbag-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 02 — What does the second degree require?
- **Accent colour** `#bf7a35`
- **Must depict** A laden supper table set for one at dusk, every dish full and untouched, with a single empty plate at the near edge.
- **Argument it carries** If he gathers what he used to eat in the forenoon to what he used to eat at night, he has not benefited from his fast. The table must be genuinely abundant and appetising — nothing here is forbidden, and nothing is being condemned. The single empty plate is the measure Ghazali actually prescribes, set against everything that is about to be added to it.

### 3. `book06-two-offices.jpg` + `book06-two-offices-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 03 — So is a bare fast a real fast?
- **Accent colour** `#c25f50`
- **Must depict** Two identical ledgers open side by side on a desk, ruled differently, each with a single entry in a different hand.
- **Argument it carries** One word doing two jobs: the jurists rule on discharge and the scholars of the hereafter mean acceptance, and neither office is the other's business. The ledgers must be *identical in binding and size* — neither is senior — and the difference must be visible only in the ruling of the pages and the hand of the entry. Both entries are correct.
