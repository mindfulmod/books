## Files

Alt text is already written into `src/book07.ts` and is what each plate has to depict. It is reproduced here as the brief. This book has four journeys.

**Set note — important.** Do not depict the Kaaba, the Sacred Mosque, Mecca, or any identifiable holy site, and include no crowds and no pilgrims. Every plate here is an ordinary object or place standing in for a stage of a journey, which is exactly how Ghazali's third chapter works: the meaning is attached to the mount, the cloth, the provision. Keep them plain, domestic, and unmistakably not the destination.

### 1. `book07-the-exchange.jpg` + `book07-the-exchange-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 01 — What is the pilgrimage actually for?
- **Accent colour** `#278d91`
- **Must depict** A stone cell cut into a hillside with its door standing open, and a worn road running past it into the distance.
- **Argument it carries** God exchanged monasticism for the pilgrimage — a permanent withdrawal replaced by one with a beginning and an end. The cell must look habitable and cared for rather than ruined, since Ghazali quotes the verse *praising* the monks; the open door and the road passing by carry the exchange. The road is the busier of the two elements.

### 2. `book07-the-creditor.jpg` + `book07-the-creditor-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 02 — What has to happen before I go?
- **Accent colour** `#bf7a35`
- **Must depict** A packed travelling roll by a doorway with a bundle of unopened letters weighted on top of it.
- **Argument it carries** Every wrong is an attachment, and every attachment is like a creditor hanging on the collar, calling out: where are you going? The roll must be *finished* — packed, tied, ready — and the letters must sit on top of it so that it cannot be lifted without dealing with them. Unopened is the point. No figure, no hand.

### 3. `book07-unstitched.jpg` + `book07-unstitched-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 03 — Which journey am I preparing for?
- **Accent colour** `#c25f50`
- **Must depict** Two lengths of plain undyed cloth folded on a shelf, both unhemmed, one slightly longer than the other.
- **Argument it carries** The consecrated garment and the shroud, and Ghazali anchors the parallel on a legal detail: neither is stitched. The unhemmed edges must be clearly visible on both — that detail *is* the argument. Both cloths are clean, plain, and of the same weave; only the length differs, and only slightly.

### 4. `book07-two-circuits.jpg` + `book07-two-circuits-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 04 — What is actually happening at the rites?
- **Accent colour** `#586fa8`
- **Must depict** A worn circular path in a stone floor around a plain square block, seen from directly above.
- **Argument it carries** Do not suppose the aim is the circling of your body around the House; the aim is the circuit of the heart. The block must be *unmistakably generic* — a plain square of undressed stone, no covering, no door, no ornament — since depicting the Kaaba is exactly what this plate must not do. The wear in the path is what shows a circuit has been made; nobody is on it.
