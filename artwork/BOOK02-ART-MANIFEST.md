Five journey plates for *The Principles of the Creed*. The app references these paths already; dropping the files in makes them appear with no code change.

## Files

Alt text is already written into `src/book02.ts` and is what each plate has to depict. It is reproduced here as the brief.

**Set note.** This book is about *handling* belief — teaching it, dosing argument, knowing what to leave unsaid. The images are therefore about care and measure rather than about doctrine. Nothing here should look like an icon or an emblem of faith.

### 1. `book02-four-holds.jpg` + `book02-four-holds-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 01 — How does belief actually begin?
- **Accent colour** `#278d91`
- **Must depict** A young sapling staked at four points by separate cords running to four fixed pegs in dry ground.
- **Argument it carries** Belief held by imitation is true and held and can be dislodged by its contrary; the remedy is not proof but four independent attachments — text, report, practice, company. There must be exactly four cords going to four separate pegs, each independently taut. A single stake, or cords converging on one point, argues the opposite.

### 2. `book02-the-dose.jpg` + `book02-the-dose-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 02 — How much argument should anyone be given?
- **Accent colour** `#bf7a35`
- **Must depict** Three apothecary vials of graduated size on a clean shelf, the smallest at front and the largest stoppered and set back.
- **Argument it carries** Theological argument as a drug: indicated by symptom, bounded in dose, with an explicit stopping rule. The largest vial must be *stoppered* and set back — it exists and it is not the default. The shelf should be orderly and unthreatening; this is a dispensary, not a warning.

### 3. `book02-the-veil.jpg` + `book02-the-veil-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 03 — Why would a true thing go unsaid?
- **Accent colour** `#c25f50`
- **Must depict** A single sheet of fine linen drawn across a lit doorway, the light passing through it evenly and no shape visible behind.
- **Argument it carries** Ghazali gives five reasons a true thing may be withheld, and then heads the conclusion with the statement that the inward agrees with the outward. The linen must be plainly *translucent* and the light behind it even and unbroken — there is no second room and no hidden shape. A veil that suggested something concealed behind it would invert the passage.

### 4. `book02-four-pillars.jpg` + `book02-four-pillars-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 04 — What does the creed actually claim?
- **Accent colour** `#586fa8`
- **Must depict** Four plain stone columns of equal height in a row, each marked with ten shallow horizontal notches.
- **Argument it carries** Four pillars of ten principles each, sorted by the kind of warrant they rest on rather than by importance. The columns must be identical in height and girth — they are not ranked — and the ten notches must be countable on each. Plain undressed stone; nothing ornamental.

### 5. `book02-three-questions.jpg` + `book02-three-questions-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 05 — Is submission the same as faith?
- **Accent colour** `#a97837`
- **Must depict** One doorway opening onto three separate corridors, each lit differently, seen from a single threshold.
- **Argument it carries** A three-sided dispute dissolves once one question is separated into a lexical, an exegetical, and a juristic one. The single threshold is the point: it must be clear that all three corridors are entered from one place. The differing light in each is what marks them as different kinds of question.
