## Files

Alt text is already written into `src/book13.ts` and is what each plate has to depict. It is reproduced here as the brief. This book has three journeys.

**Set note.** No people and no transactions in progress — the argument is that a completed and valid deal can still be a wrong, so every plate shows an aftermath or an instrument rather than an exchange. Keep the commercial setting plain and prosperous: nothing here is a critique of trade, which the first chapter places among the blessings.

### 1. `book13-two-standards.jpg` + `book13-two-standards-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 01 — Is permitted the same as right?
- **Accent colour** `#278d91`
- **Must depict** A signed and sealed contract lying on a counter beside a small set of unequal weights.
- **Argument it carries** A dealing the jurist rules valid and concluded may still contain a wrong, since not every prohibition voids a contract. The document must look *properly executed* — signed, sealed, unambiguous — and the weights beside it visibly mismatched. Two standards in one frame, and the paper satisfies only the first.

### 2. `book13-capital-profit.jpg` + `book13-capital-profit-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 02 — Is being fair the whole of it?
- **Accent colour** `#bf7a35`
- **Must depict** An open ledger with a column of figures and a second column beside it, only the first column totalled.
- **Argument it carries** Justice runs in trade as capital runs; excellence runs as profit runs — and nobody who trades regards recovering his capital as a good year. The first column must be neatly ruled off and totalled, the second column present, filled in, and *never summed*. No legible words; figures and rules only.

### 3. `book13-two-markets.jpg` + `book13-two-markets-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 03 — What is my trade doing to me?
- **Accent colour** `#c25f50`
- **Must depict** A shuttered market stall at midday with the street beyond it empty, and an open doorway further down the same street.
- **Argument it carries** The market of this world must not prevent a man from the market of the hereafter — and the markets of the hereafter are the mosques. Both must be on *one street*, in the same light, neither idealised: the stall shuttered and orderly, the far doorway simply open. The distance between them is the whole plate.
