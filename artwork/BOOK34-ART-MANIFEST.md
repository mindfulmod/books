# Book 34 art manifest

Five journey plates for *Poverty and Abstinence*. The app references these paths already; dropping the files in makes them appear with no code change.

## Specs

| | |
|---|---|
| Serving directory | `public/assets/system/` |
| Master directory | `artwork/system-source/` (full-resolution PNG) |
| Full size | 1600 × 900 JPEG (16:9) |
| Thumbnail | 480 × 270 JPEG |
| Thumbnail path | derived automatically by replacing `.jpg` with `-thumb.jpg` — both files are required |
| Crop safety | the extreme top and bottom are cropped on narrow viewports; keep the load-bearing content out of those bands |

## Files

Alt text is already written into `src/book34.ts` and is what each plate has to depict. It is reproduced here as the brief.

### 1. `book34-five-states.jpg` + `book34-five-states-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 01 — What kind of not-having is mine?
- **Accent colour** `#278d91`
- **Must depict** Five identical ivory alcoves along a sunlit wall, each holding an empty brass bowl turned to a slightly different angle.
- **Argument it carries** Poverty is one outward condition covering five distinct inward states, and the state is the whole difference. The bowls must be identically empty and identically made; only their orientation varies. If any bowl looks fuller or finer than another, the image argues that circumstances differ, which is exactly what the chapter denies.

### 2. `book34-matched-pair.jpg` + `book34-matched-pair-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 02 — Is it better to have less?
- **Accent colour** `#586fa8`
- **Must depict** A bright marble court where two identical brass scales stand level, one bearing a full vessel and one an empty one.
- **Argument it carries** Ghazali refuses to rank having and not-having in the abstract and compares only matched cases. Both scales must be exactly level — this is the single most important instruction in the plate. A tipped scale answers a question the chapter declines to answer.

### 3. `book34-open-hand.jpg` + `book34-open-hand-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 03 — May I take it, and may I ask?
- **Accent colour** `#c25f50`
- **Must depict** An ivory threshold where a covered dish rests unattended beside a closed door and an open one.
- **Argument it carries** Receiving what arrives and going out to seek it are governed by different rules. The dish must read as delivered and left, with no one present; the two doors must be equally well-kept so that neither is the obviously virtuous one. The composition asks a question rather than answering it.

### 4. `book34-the-exchange.jpg` + `book34-the-exchange-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 04 — What does detachment actually mean?
- **Accent colour** `#bf7a35`
- **Must depict** A sunlit market sill where one hand's worth of coins lies beside a sealed letter, the coins pushed slightly away.
- **Argument it carries** Abstinence is a trade rather than a loss: something is let go because something better is expected, which is why it is not the same as poverty. The letter must read as valuable and unopened, and the push must be small and deliberate. Nothing should look discarded or spoiled.

### 5. `book34-the-measure.jpg` + `book34-the-measure-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 05 — What about what I cannot give up?
- **Accent colour** `#a97837`
- **Must depict** A bright pantry sill holding six graduated vessels, each filled to a different marked line, with a plain cloth folded beside them.
- **Argument it carries** Where a thing cannot be left, the exchange moves to its measure: food, clothing, dwelling, and furnishing are each held to a line rather than surrendered. The marks must be visible and the fills must genuinely differ. The folded cloth carries the same argument in the register of plainness rather than absence.
