# Book 40 art manifest

Five journey plates for *Remembrance of Death and the Afterlife* — the last book of the Ihya. The app references these paths already; dropping the files in makes them appear with no code change.

## Specs

| | |
|---|---|
| Serving directory | `public/assets/system/` |
| Master directory | `artwork/system-source/` (full-resolution PNG) |
| Full size | 1600 × 900 JPEG (16:9) |
| Thumbnail | 480 × 270 JPEG |
| Thumbnail path | derived automatically by replacing `.jpg` with `-thumb.jpg` — both files are required |
| Crop safety | the extreme top and bottom are cropped on narrow viewports; keep the load-bearing content out of those bands |

## Files

Alt text is already written into `src/book40.ts` and is what each plate has to depict. It is reproduced here as the brief.

**Tone note for the whole set.** This book ends on a chapter about the breadth of God's mercy — Ghazali says so at the head of its second half — and its last line is that God is more merciful to His servants than a nursing mother to her child. None of these five plates should be macabre, dim, or funereal. Every one is lit plainly and every one is empty of people. The subject is preparation and proportion, not dread.

### 1. `book40-the-traveller.jpg` + `book40-the-traveller-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 01 — Why does remembering death change nothing?
- **Accent colour** `#278d91`
- **Must depict** A packed travelling bag and a water-skin set by an open door, with an empty road running out into pale distance beyond it.
- **Argument it carries** The remembrance fails because it lands on a heart occupied by appetite; the remedy is the condition of a man about to cross a perilous waste, who thinks of one thing because one thing is genuinely in front of him. The bag must read as *packed and ready*, not abandoned. Ghazali's traveller is not frightened — the plate wants focus, not menace.

### 2. `book40-two-brothers.jpg` + `book40-two-brothers-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 02 — Where is the time actually going?
- **Accent colour** `#bf7a35`
- **Must depict** A doorway laid for one arrival — a single place set, a made bed — and a second identical room beyond it, unprepared and dark.
- **Argument it carries** Two absent brothers, one expected tomorrow and one in a year; both loved, both certain, and only the near one produces preparation. The two rooms must be *identical in every respect* except readiness, because the analogy holds affection and certainty constant and varies only nearness. If the second room looks like a lesser room, the plate argues the wrong thing.

### 3. `book40-last-words.jpg` + `book40-last-words-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 03 — What actually happens at the end?
- **Accent colour** `#c25f50`
- **Must depict** A low cushion set upright against a wall in an empty room, a shallow cup beside it, late light across the floor.
- **Argument it carries** Muʿawiya asked to be seated, praised God, wept, and prayed for mercy on a hard heart. The cushion propped upright is the whole image — someone was helped to sit up here. The room must be plainly furnished and the light warm and late. No bed, no shroud, no mourning.

### 4. `book40-change-of-state.jpg` + `book40-change-of-state-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 04 — What is death, and what is the grave?
- **Accent colour** `#586fa8`
- **Must depict** A sealed clay lamp and an unsealed one side by side on a stone ledge, identical in shape, one lit.
- **Argument it carries** Death means only a change of state; the spirit remains after separating from the body. The two lamps must be unmistakably the same object in two conditions — same clay, same form, same size. The unlit one must not read as broken or spent. The definition is deflationary on purpose, and the plate has to be too.

### 5. `book40-the-seal.jpg` + `book40-the-seal-thumb.jpg`
- **Journey** 05 — What can be known, and how does it end?
- **Accent colour** `#a97837`
- **Must depict** A pair of brass scales at rest, one pan holding a thick stack of folded papers, the other a single small card, and the card is down.
- **Argument it carries** The last movement of the entire Ihya: scrolls of misdeeds in one pan, one card bearing the testimony in the other, and the scrolls fly up. The imbalance must be decisive and slightly absurd — the disproportion is the point. This is the closing plate of the whole forty-book edition, so it should be the warmest and most settled image in the set.
